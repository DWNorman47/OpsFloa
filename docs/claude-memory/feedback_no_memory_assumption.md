**David does not remember what he asked for last week, and he does not remember what you proposed.** He knows his app cold. Those are different things, and confusing them has failed twice.

In his words: *"start from the beginning without assuming a flawless memory from me. I know my app, but I don't rememebr what I asked for last week."* And: *"You gave me a huge response last time that I couldn't understand, and when I asked for help, you gave me a rundown of my app and everything I know, completely ignoring the things you brought up last time."*

**Why:** every session name you invent — "GC Tools", "the ESC pack", "the installed-price trap", "the standouts" — is *your* shorthand, not shared vocabulary. It feels established to you because you coined it an hour ago; to him it's a term he's never seen, attached to a proposal he doesn't recall. When he asked *"'You've already built half of it.' Half of what? What is GC Tools, what does it consist of?"*, the failure wasn't that the answer was wrong — it was that it assumed a conversation he wasn't carrying.

**How to apply:**
- **Define your own terms before using them, every time.** If you named it, he doesn't know it. One clause is enough: "GC tools — a list of ~30 ideas for general contractors that you and I brainstormed."
- **When he asks for an explanation, explain what *you* raised** — the proposal, the finding, the decision waiting on him. **Do not summarize his app back to him.** He built it. Re-narrating Projects/Field/Takeoff to him is the exact move that frustrated him.
- **Answer the question he actually asked.** Not the adjacent one, not the fuller version you'd rather answer.
- **Length is a bug.** A response he has to re-read, or that makes him ask for help understanding it, has already failed no matter how correct it is. Lead with the answer; cut the supporting material to what changes his next move.
- **Written docs are how continuity survives, not his memory** — that's what [[feedback_worklog]] and [[project_backlog_doc]] are for. Put the durable thing in the doc; keep the message short.
